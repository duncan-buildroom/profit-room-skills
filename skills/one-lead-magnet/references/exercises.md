# The 10 exercises

Mirrors the Lead Magnet Workbook. For each exercise: the one-line why, what to pre-fill from sources, the questions (ask ONE at a time, always with the example), your recommendation rule, and the block to save in `answers.md`.

Examples below are samples to show the member what a good answer looks like. Swap in an example from the member's own field once you know it. Never present an example as their answer.

---

## PART 1: RESEARCH

### 1. Buyer questions
Why: do not pick the topic from your own head. Pick it from your customers' mouths. Method credit: Caleb Ralston's "Painful Problems" exercise.
Pre-fill: scan sources for questions buyers asked BEFORE buying (calls, DMs, comments, reviews, emails). Quote them as "I" statements, with a count of how many times each appeared. If you found some, show them and ask what is missing.
Ask:
- If nothing was found: "What do your ideal customers ask you about the problem you solve, before they buy? Give me as many as you remember, in their words. Like: 'Do I really need surgery?' or 'How long until I see results?'" Keep asking "what else do they ask?" until you have about 15 or they run dry.
- "Which of these have you heard more than once?" One mention is a story, three is a topic.
Rules: only questions a real person said. If they get stuck under 7, tell them honestly that listening to more customers first will make the magnet better, and offer to pause. Do not invent questions to pad the list.
Recommend: the repeats, ranked by how often they came up.
Save: list of questions (with times heard), then "Repeats: ...".

### 2. The problem before your offer
Why: the topic is the problem that sits right before your offer. Solve it free, and the next problem it reveals is the one your offer solves.
Pre-fill: offer from the brand profile or sources.
Ask:
- "What do you sell, and who is it for, in one line? Like: 'A free movement screen for adults with knee pain.'" (skip if the profile has it)
- For the top repeat: "What problem sits behind this question? Like: 'Do I need surgery?' sits on top of 'I don't know where my knee stands or what to do first.'"
- "Once they solve that, what is the next problem, the one you help with?"
Recommend: the single narrowest problem a buyer would feel, that points at the offer. Run the check: a real person said it, it is one problem not two, giving it away free will not hurt the business, the next problem points to the offer.
Save: offer, problem right before the offer, next problem, how the offer solves it.

### 3. Competitor gap
Why: you are looking for what others leave out, not for something to copy.
Pre-fill: if the member named competitors in sources, use those. Offer to look up their free resources with a web search or by opening their site (tell the member what you looked at).
Ask:
- "Name up to three people who sell to the same buyer. Like: the other physio clinic across town, or a well-known blogger in your field." Offer to find them if they do not know.
- For each: what is their free resource (name and format), and what does it skip? Fill this in yourself from their pages where you can, then ask the member to confirm or correct.
- "What do your customers keep asking you about that those resources skip?"
Recommend: the gap where the member can go deeper, be more specific to one buyer, or add proof and a story the others lack. If the idea only works because it would be prettier, say so and keep looking.
Save: table of who / free resource / what it skips, then "The gap I can fill: ...".

### 4. One-sentence promise
Why: if you cannot say it simply, a buyer cannot understand it. Everything built later is checked against this sentence.
Shape: "For [who] who wants [result], this shows [one thing]."
Ask (draft it from exercises 1 to 3 first, then ask them to confirm each part):
- "Who is it for? One kind of buyer. Like: 'adults with knee pain'."
- "What result do they want, in their words? Like: 'avoid a rushed surgery decision'."
- "What is the one thing this shows them? Like: 'what to check, what to do first and when to get it looked at'."
Test: if the sentence needs an "and" between two problems, it is two magnets. Recommend the stronger half and cut the other.
Save: the three parts and the final sentence.

## PART 2: DESIGN

### 5. Pick the format
Why: the format should match the job the buyer needs done.
Formats:
- Roadmap: shows where they stand and what comes next. Best when the problem is "I don't know where to start." Service businesses usually do best with a roadmap that has a short self-check.
- Workbook: exercises that end with their own answers. Best when the result comes from thinking it through.
- Scorecard or quiz: a few questions that score them. Best when the problem is hidden.
- Template or checklist: saves time on one task. Light, so use it as a section inside a bigger piece, not the whole magnet.
Ask with AskUserQuestion: 4 options (put the recommended one first and label it "(recommended)"), with the one-line "best when" as each description. Then: "How long should it take to finish, and roughly how many pages? Like: 20 minutes, 10 pages." Recommend short: as short as the result allows, still worth paying for.
Save: format, why it fits (two lines), target length.

### 6. Outline and titles
Why: outline the whole thing on one page before writing a word. Every section ends with one thing the reader can do.
Required parts: cover and title / Start here: a two-minute self-check / four to six steps, each ending in one thing to do / when it does not work or when to get help / last page: one next step and one link.
Do: draft the full outline yourself from the promise, show it as a table (part, working title, the one thing the reader does, pages), then ask: "Anything here that solves a second problem? Say which and I will cut it." Cut anything that does.
Titles: write 5, naming the result not the topic. Use AskUserQuestion to pick one (recommend one, say why in a line). The member can type their own.
Save: the outline table, the 5 titles, the chosen title.

## PART 3: BUILD AND LAUNCH

### 7. Draft
This exercise is the build itself, see "Build the lead magnet" in SKILL.md. Before drafting ask:
- "What stories, examples or tips do you say on every first call? Like: 'the three things I tell everyone to stop doing.'" Use these as raw material in every section.
- Regulated field? Ask: "Is this health, legal or financial? If so, who in your business reviews each claim before it goes out?" Record the reviewer's name. In these fields add a "general information, not advice" line to the first content page and the last page, and keep the claims general.
Pay test at the end: "Would you pay $20 for this?" If not, add depth, not pages: name the section you would deepen.
Save: stories supplied, regulated yes/no, reviewer, section status (drafted / approved).

### 8. Proof bank
Why: a magnet full of fluff makes people think you have nothing else. Proof makes them trust it.
Pre-fill: reviews, wins and numbers from sources and the profile's Proof section. List each with where it came from.
Ask:
- "What real results, reviews or short stories from your work can we use? Only things that actually happened. Like: a patient review you have permission to share, or 'I've done this for 40 clients' only if that number is true."
- For each: "Do you have permission to use it?" Mark OK to use yes or no.
- "What do you NOT want to claim? Anything you cannot stand behind stays out."
- "What is the story you tell on every first call?" (if not already given in 7)
Rules: never invent a result, quote, name or number. If they have no proof, use their own method, process or honest "here is what I see in my work" lines, and say what is missing. Offer the proof-kit skill if installed.
Then place each piece: which section, which email.
Save: proof table (proof, facts, source/permission, goes in section, OK), claims left out.

### 9. Your path
Why: links in captions do not click on Instagram and cost reach on LinkedIn, so the post asks for one comment word. Comment, then a DM with a link, then an email form, then 5 emails, then the offer.
Ask:
- "Where do you post most? Like: Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook." Use that for the tool pick.
- Keyword: suggest one (easy to type, matches the topic, one word, not 'roadmap123'). "Does KNEE work, or do you want a different word?"
- Tool: AskUserQuestion, recommend by platform: ManyChat for Instagram and Facebook, LeadShark for LinkedIn, Blotato for Instagram and Facebook plus scheduling. Tell them to check each pricing page, plans change. Say plainly that this skill writes the text, the tool runs the automation.
- Email tool: "Which email tool do you use, or will you use? Like: Kit, Mailchimp, MailerLite." If none, recommend a free plan.
- "Where will the PDF live so it opens with no login, and what is the link? (Leave blank if you do not have it yet, I will put a marker.)"
- "What is your offer's link? Like: ridgelinept.com/movement-screen."
Then write the whole launch kit per `references/launch-kit.md`, show the DM and email 1 for a quick OK, and save the rest.
Save: path line (post topic / keyword / DM / email / offer), tool, email tool, PDF link, offer link.

### 10. Weekly scorecard
Why: the job after launch is making this ONE magnet better, not making a second. Give it a month before judging.
Do: put the scorecard table and the leak guide in `launch-kit.md` (format in `references/launch-kit.md`). Explain in two lines how to use it: read left to right, the first stop that drops hard is the leak, fix one thing a week. Ask: "What day of the week will you fill it in? Like: Monday." Add that day to the file.
Save: scorecard day.
